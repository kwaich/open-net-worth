# Importing a statement during onboarding

Read this before committing any statement the user sends while setting up.

- Before committing a statement, check its own-account transfers and ask which destination wallets or bank accounts belong to them. Create those accounts and their actual starting balances first, then route each such row as a transfer in the import decision itself (`to_account_id`), so it is booked once with both legs — never import it as spending and patch it afterwards. Never book a PayLah top-up or a move to their Revolut as spending just because that destination account does not exist yet.
- Do not invent a destination or its opening balance — but do not stall either: ask for everything the import needs (which destinations are theirs, each one's balance and as of when) in ONE short question. When they don't know a balance, take their best estimate or today's balance as a provisional opening, say once that it is provisional and where to correct it, and import.
- For each flagged statement row they approve, pass an explicit create decision on that same statement and verify it booked; never repair a skipped row by hand-entering it outside the import batch.
- An account opened at today's balance (the usual way) keeps it: statement rows dated before its Starting Balance are history and don't change the balance, so there is nothing to re-date or re-amount — the import preview says so. Only an account created from the statement itself, with no balance for today, starts at the statement's opening balance dated just before its first row; say then that today's figure is right only once every later statement is in. Brokerage openings may summarize earlier activity; do not rewind them without reconciling positions and cash.
- After import, check the displayed balances of the source and destination accounts are what you expect: unchanged by rows dated before an account's Starting Balance, moved by rows on or after it. A statement net tie alone proves the rows, not the balance.
- Rows dated before an account's Starting Balance are history: the budget counts only from that date, but Dashboard › Reports (savings rate, spending trends, income vs expense, money flow) and Plan › Cash Flow (each month's in and out, its transactions under the month) include them. When the user asks where to see that spending again, point them there, and give the month's category breakdown in your reply.
- Then categorize what you imported in the same pass — suggest categories for the new rows' payees and apply the clear ones, so their spending means something on day one — and mention only how many are left for them to review, not each row.
- Income is part of that pass, not only spending. A fresh book has no income categories at all (its starter categories are all expenses), so a salary or interest credit stays Uncategorized, the briefing then asks the user to tag it and Money flow shows "Uncategorized". Call list_categories: when there is no income category for the pay or the interest, create "Salary" and "Interest" (kind `income`) with create_categories, then label the obvious credits with them: a payroll or salary credit as Salary, bank interest as Interest. Leave anything ambiguous (a transfer from a person, a refund, a deposit you cannot name) for the user, and say in one line what you labelled.
